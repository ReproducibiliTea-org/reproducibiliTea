Research progresses through feedback and evaluation, and transparent reviews are valuable to the research landscape!

By writing post-publication reviews or comments, journal clubs can learn together about peer review while engaging directly with other researchers. You can publish peer reviews online anonymously or with your name(s) using several different platforms. Writing your journal club discussion points online can contextualise research for other readers, provide input for authors to develop or correct their work, and give you credit for your expertise.

## How to review

Principles and examples for preprint reviews or comments: [ASAPbio - Fast Principles](https://asapbio.org/focus-areas/peer-review/fast-principles/) | [OSF](https://osf.io/9wdcq)

Early career researchers guide to reviewing: [ECR Reviewers Platform](https://ecr-reviewers.gitlab.io/guide/)

**Example:**
> "This review was conducted by journal club members [at (name of the institution)]. We are giving feedback specifically on the methods and statistical analyses reported in this article. This study investigated…and reports findings of... We provide feedback to strengthen the [methodology/theoretical basis/results] and to [provide context/highlight future research/clarify]…"

## What to review

* Articles that you are already discussing as part of your journal club
* Metaresearch articles, e.g., from our list of suggested review targets [link to the collated list]
* Preprints or published articles in an area where you have expertise or interest as a group

## Where to post

* [PREreview](https://prereview.org/) for preprints, for a DOI and recognition on your ORCID profile
* Comments section on a journal website, preprint server, or publishing platform (if available)
* [PubPeer](https://pubpeer.com/), for widest visibility and ease of use
* [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/), to gain a DOI
* Personal or journal club blog

## Things to consider

* **What is the level of expertise in your group?** You can make this clear in your comment by stating that members are early career researchers, or by describing the areas of research that you work in.
* **Would you prefer to be anonymous?** Reviews on PREreview, PubPeer, and other platforms can be posted anonymously. It may be preferable to withhold your identity if you have concerns about being identified as the reviewer, but it does mean that you cannot receive credit for the work of reviewing.
* **What is the scope of your review/comment?** If you only review/comment on a specific aspect of the article or error that you find, remember to state that.
* **Is your comment written in an appropriate tone?** Is it respectful, constructive, and professional? Does it discuss the research in a non-speculative way, and align with the FAST principles?
